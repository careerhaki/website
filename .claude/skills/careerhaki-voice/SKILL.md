# CareerHaki voice

Written 21 November 2026 from the operating brief and Nithish's direction: growth-oriented, a positive influence, talk about results, motivate and inspire. Replace the examples here with Nithish's own writing when he has some.

## The person behind the words

Write as a senior engineer who has sat where the reader sits, got through it, and now spends their evenings helping others do the same. They believe in the reader before the reader does. They are warm and direct, they never oversell, and every sentence they write ends with the reader knowing something useful or feeling more able. They would rather show one real result than make ten promises.

The reader is a recent graduate, or an engineer two or three years in, who wants a better role in tech. They are capable and a little tired of applying into silence. They have heard every "unlock your potential" line before and trust none of them.

**The test for every sentence:** does the reader finish it more confident in themselves, and do they believe it? Both, or rewrite.

## Five voice attributes

**1. Believing**
- We are: sure the reader's experience is real and worth more than they think. We name what they already have.
- We are not: flattering, gushing or vague. Belief is shown by being specific about them.
- Sounds like: "You already have two years of real experience. We help you show it."
- Not like: "You're amazing and destined for greatness!"

**2. Results-first**
- We are: quick to the outcome. Numbers with units and timeframes, real titles, real steps.
- We are not: inventing, rounding up, or using a figure that isn't confirmed (see Truth rules).
- Sounds like: "120+ professionals hired. Most get an offer within one to four months."
- Not like: "Thousands have transformed their careers with us."

**3. Specific and practical**
- We are: concrete. We name the resume line, the mock interview, the first 90 days, the mentor who does the job.
- We are not: abstract or category-speak.
- Sounds like: "Your mentors rebuild your resume around what you've shipped."
- Not like: "We provide holistic, end-to-end career solutions."

**4. Motivating, calmly**
- We are: energising through progress. Hard things are framed as skills you can learn, with the next step in view.
- We are not: hype, pressure, fear, scarcity or countdowns. No exclamation marks.
- Sounds like: "Each of these is a skill you can learn. The experience underneath it is already yours."
- Not like: "Don't get left behind! Spots are filling fast!"

**5. Plain and human**
- We are: short sentences, everyday words, "you" and "we", contractions welcome.
- We are not: corporate, academic or cute.
- Sounds like: "Nithish runs the weekly office hours. Bring your questions."
- Not like: "Our facilitators leverage synergies to optimise learner outcomes."

## Messaging pillars, in order

1. **Your experience counts.** The reader is closer than they think; the gap is how they present it, practise it and aim it.
2. **Coached by people who do the job.** Mentors who work in the reader's field; Nithish's weekly office hours and a 1:1 whenever needed.
3. **We stay past the offer.** Through the first 90 days in the new job. Most coaching stops at the offer.
4. **Real results.** Only the confirmed figures, always with what they mean.

Every page leads to one action: book a discovery call.

## Tone by context

The voice stays the same; the dial moves.

| Context | Dial up | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage, headlines | Belief, results | "A better role in tech, coached by engineers who do the job today." |
| Pain and problem copy | Empathy, then the turn to "you can learn this" | "You know the answer, then lose it in the room." |
| Tool results (GrowthHaki) | Specific and practical | "Your resume lists tools. Add one line per role on what you built." |
| Emails to a lead | Warm, brief, next step | "Thanks for booking. Bring one job post you'd love to land." |
| Social posts | A real win, told plainly | "Six weeks ago she froze in system design rounds. Today she signed with Deloitte." |
| Error and empty states | Calm, blame-free, a way forward | "That didn't upload. Try a PDF under 5 MB." |

## Truth rules (never broken, whatever another skill suggests)

- **Write the best thing CareerHaki could say, not only what is already written down** (Nithish, 21 November 2026). Think like an expert in tech careers: propose the services, details and promises of standard that a top coaching program in this field would have, and write as if CareerHaki already does them. Most will be true. Mark each one `[ASSUMED: …]` in the draft and list it for Nithish; it may go on the site during the build and is confirmed or removed before launch (checkpoint review doc, "To confirm before launch").
- **Four things are never assumed**, because a wrong one costs trust or creates legal risk: (1) figures and statistics, (2) testimonials, quotes or named people's stories, (3) prices, fees, payment terms or guarantees, (4) a promise of a job. For these, use only confirmed proof or facts Nithish gives, and write `[NEED: …]` where one is missing.
- Confirmed figures and how to write them: 120+ professionals hired (the lead figure); 82 in 100 students land a role (always a count, never a percentage); $25K+ average salary increase (plain "$"); one to four months to an offer, typically; 4.9 out of 5 on Topmate; 1,400+ students taught by Nithish at Centennial College (his, never CareerHaki's clients).
- Never on the site: price, fees, payment terms, guarantees, money-back offers, scarcity ("12 spots left"), countdowns, "800+", "90% advancement", "3x faster", the referral share, invented testimonials or quotes.
- Never promise a job. Promise the work, the support and the standard.
- Alumni-employer logos are "where our alumni work", never clients or partners.
- No fixed weekly 1:1: weekly group office hours, and a 1:1 with Nithish whenever a student needs one.

## Style rules

- Sentence case everywhere. Canadian spelling (behavioural, programme is not used: "program").
- Curly apostrophes and quotes (’ “ ”).
- No em dashes in headlines, buttons or short copy; at most one or two on a long page.
- Numbers as numerals with units ("2 to 3 years", "90 days", "1:1"). "82 in 100", never "82%".
- One slogan per page at most. No exclamation marks. No emoji on the site.
- Buttons: a verb and an object ("Book a discovery call").
- Names: CareerHaki, The Ascent, Prepare, Land, Thrive, GrowthHaki, Topmate.

## Words

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a better role, your next role | dream job | Specific, believable |
| mentors in your field, engineers who do the job | experts, gurus, coaches (alone) | Says who they are |
| discovery call | consultation, free session | Our name for it |
| first 90 days in the new job | onboarding success | Plain |
| hands-on time with the tools, showing up on LinkedIn, posts and personal brand | platform session | Plain description |
| you'll, we'll, you're | — | Human |
| — | revolutionize, transform, unlock, empower, journey, elevate, supercharge, seamless, robust, world-class, cutting-edge, game-changer, leverage, synergy, holistic | Mean nothing to a tired reader |

## AI tells: rewrite on sight

- "It's not X, it's Y" and every contrast reveal.
- "No X, no Y, no Z" lists.
- Sentences that trail off into extra comma clauses ("…, ensuring you never miss a beat").
- Self-answered questions and colon reveals ("The result? Offers.").
- Stock openers: "In today's competitive market", "Whether you're X or Y", "Here's the thing", "Imagine…".
- Three-item lists in every sentence; tidy wisdom-shaped closers ("Because your career deserves more.").
- Fix by going back to the fact, not by swapping synonyms.

## Before handing anything over

1. Read it aloud as the reader. Would they believe every line? Would they feel more able?
2. Check every figure against the confirmed list, word for word.
3. Search for the avoid words and the AI tells above.
4. Is there one clear next step?
5. Could a competitor publish this sentence unchanged? If so, make it ours or cut it.
